How to build a simple two-step skincare routine

You don’t need ten products. A cleanser and a moisturizer that suit your skin are a solid start. Here’s how to choose each one, what to check before you buy, and what to try if your moisturizer pills.

Step 1: Pick a cleanser for your skin type

Start with the skin types the product lists. A cleanser marked for your skin type is a safer first choice than one picked for its popularity.

Gel, cream or balm: what the texture tells you

Gel cleansers usually feel light and rinse clean. Some lather a lot and some barely at all, so check reviews if foam matters to you. The fresh Soy cleanser, for example, is a gel that reviewers describe as low-lather.

Removing makeup: when one cleanse is enough

If you wear light makeup, one cleanse may be enough. The brand says the fresh Soy cleanser removes eye makeup, including mascara, though some reviewers found it weak on heavier makeup. If you wear a full face, plan for a first cleanse that breaks makeup down.

Why a moisturizer can pill, and what to try

Pilling is when a cream rolls into small flakes as you rub it in. Some reviewers report it, especially when a rich cream goes over a serum or retinol that hasn’t dried. If it happens to you, try:

  • a thinner layer

  • letting each step dry before the next

  • pressing the cream in instead of rubbing

These don’t fix pilling for everyone. If it keeps happening on clean skin, the product may not suit you.

What to check before you buy

A lot of disappointment in reviews comes from things you can check in advance:

  • Scent. Some products have a noticeable scent. Fragrance-free products say so on the label.

  • Texture. Rich creams feel heavier and may pill under makeup.

  • Price per size. All three products here list a mini size, so you can try before the full size.

Patch-test a new product first

Some reviewers report breakouts or irritation after starting a new product. Try a small amount on one area for a few days before using it on your whole face. If you have a skin condition, talk to a dermatologist.
